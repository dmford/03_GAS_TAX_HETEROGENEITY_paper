# Gas Tax Heterogeneity and Consumer Response

This research project examines why consumers respond differently to gasoline price changes across U.S. states and how local characteristics influence those behavioral responses.

Using state-level elasticity estimates, demographic data, transportation infrastructure measures, and geographic characteristics, the analysis investigates heterogeneous treatment effects in fuel demand and evaluates how policy responses vary across locations.

The project combines applied microeconomics, policy evaluation, and observational data analysis to better understand cross-state variation in consumer behavior.

## Main Finding

Consumer responses to gasoline price changes vary substantially across states.

States with denser populations, more urbanized transportation networks, and greater access to neighboring fuel markets tend to exhibit stronger responses to gasoline price changes, while states with favorable weather conditions and larger geographic areas tend to exhibit weaker responses.

The results suggest that local conditions play an important role in shaping fuel demand and that policy impacts can vary meaningfully across locations.

## Methodology

The analysis examines heterogeneous treatment effects using state-level variation in gasoline price elasticity estimates.

Key empirical approaches include:

* Cross-sectional econometric analysis
* Heterogeneity analysis
* Policy evaluation
* Observational data integration
* Robustness checks across alternative specifications

The project evaluates how demographic, geographic, environmental, and transportation-related characteristics explain variation in observed consumer responses to gasoline price changes.

## Data

The project combines:

* State-specific gasoline price elasticity estimates
* Demographic characteristics
* Transportation infrastructure measures
* Geographic and environmental variables
* Cross-state policy and market characteristics

The resulting dataset allows for the systematic evaluation of factors associated with heterogeneity in fuel demand responses.

## Research Motivation

Many policy evaluations focus on average treatment effects, even when policy impacts may differ substantially across populations and locations.

This project examines the sources of that variation by identifying characteristics associated with stronger or weaker consumer responses to gasoline price changes. Understanding these differences can improve policy design and lead to more accurate forecasts of policy effectiveness.
